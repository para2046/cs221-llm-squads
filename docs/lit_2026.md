## (a) LLM agents in StarCraft II / SMAC

**COMPASS: Closed-Loop Vision-Language Planning for Multi-Agent Coordination** (`li2025compass`), Z. Li, 2025, arXiv 2502.10148, preprint.
*Method:* decentralised VLM agents write and refine code-based skills held in a library bootstrapped from expert demonstrations. Entity information spreads through a structured multi-hop communication protocol.
*Result:* on SMACv2 it beats MARL baselines; on symmetric Protoss 5v5 it reaches a **57% win rate vs QMIX's 27%**.
*Relation:* this is the **closest quantitative comparator**. Same benchmark and same race/size; its 57% is close to our C2 60% (31B), though we have not checked whether difficulty and protocol match. Its "structured entity communication" is very close to our C1 state reports, so C1 is a strong precedent rather than a novelty. Differences: we use plain per-step text actions with no code, no demonstrations and no skill library, and we isolate the channel's causal effect with C0/C1/C2 ablations. COMPASS does not report such ablations in its abstract.

**SMAC-Hard: Mixed Opponent Strategy Script and Self-play on SMAC** (`deng2024smachard`), Y. Deng, 2024, arXiv 2412.17707, preprint.
*Method:* replaces SMAC's single built-in AI with editable and mixed opponent scripts, and adds a self-play interface.
*Result:* MARL algorithms overfit and exploit the default opponent, and they struggle against edited or mixed scripts.
*Relation:* this supports a caveat for us: win rate against the built-in AI may reflect exploits of one opponent. It also supports our self-play setup, and the 61/88 side-A asymmetry is the kind of artefact its black-box testing would expose.

**StarWM: World Models for Policy Refinement in StarCraft II** (`zhang2026starwm`), Y. Zhang, 2026, arXiv 2602.14857, preprint.
*Method:* a learned, action-conditioned textual world model predicts short-horizon observations, used in a Generate-Simulate-Refine loop for full-game SC2.
*Result:* win-rate gains of +30/+15/+30 points against built-in AI at Hard/Harder/VeryHard. It is complementary to history-summarisation agents, and combining the two works best.
*Relation:* orthogonal (macro, single agent). Its finding that history and foresight help matches our E4B history ablation (19% to 3% without the 5-step history).

**HIMA: Society of Mind Meets Real-Time Strategy** (`ahn2025hima`), D. Ahn, 2025, arXiv 2508.06042, COLM 2025.
*Method:* specialised imitation-learned LLM agents propose multistep plans, and a Strategic Planner merges them. Also introduces the TEXTSCII-ALL testbed.
*Result:* better strategic clarity, adaptability and efficiency than prior state of the art (qualitative claims in the abstract).
*Relation:* uses a hierarchical planner where we use flat peers. Our 31B never produced higher-level plans; HIMA hard-codes that level instead of hoping it emerges.

**SC2Arena and StarEvolve** (`shen2025sc2arena`), P. Shen, 2025, arXiv 2508.10428, preprint. *Method:* a full-game benchmark covering all races, plus a Planner-Executor-Verifier agent fine-tuned on high-quality self-play data selected by a scoring system. *Relation:* their "fine-tune on scored own games" is a self-distillation recipe close to our planned AWR on 31B games. Their setting is macro, while ours is micro.

## (b) Multi-agent RL post-training and distillation for LLM agents

**ReMA: Learning to Meta-think with Multi-Agent RL** (`wan2025rema`), Z. Wan, 2025, arXiv 2503.09501, preprint.
*Method:* a high-level meta-thinking agent and a low-level reasoning agent are trained with MARL and aligned objectives. The multi-turn version uses turn-level ratios and parameter sharing.
*Result:* beats single-agent RL baselines on competition maths and LLM-as-judge benchmarks.
*Relation:* explicit planner/executor roles trained jointly. This is a possible fix for our "no higher-level plans" finding: assign one unit a commander role. Like us, it uses parameter sharing across roles.

**Self-RedTeam: Online Self-Play RL for Safer LMs** (`liu2025selfredteam`), M. Liu, 2025, arXiv 2506.07468, ICML 2026.
*Method:* one policy plays both attacker and defender in a zero-sum game with online MARL, with hidden CoT per role.
*Result:* +17.8% attack diversity (SBERT) and up to 95% better safety across 14 benchmarks.
*Relation:* the same single-policy, two-role zero-sum self-play we use, and it **works** at their scale. Their roles are asymmetric by design, while our sides are nominally symmetric but empirically asymmetric (A wins 61/88). That asymmetry may bias our GRPO groups.

**Agent Distillation: Distilling LLM Agents into Small Models with Tools** (`kang2025agentdistill`), M. Kang, 2025, arXiv 2505.17612, NeurIPS 2025 (spotlight).
*Method:* distils full agent trajectories (not only CoT) from a teacher. A "first-thought prefix" improves teacher trajectories, and self-consistent action generation adds robustness at test time.
*Result:* 0.5B-3B students match next-tier 1.5B-7B models trained with CoT distillation.
*Relation:* direct support for our next step (distil E4B from 31B game trajectories). Self-consistent action sampling is cheap to try at inference for E4B.

**MiniLLM: On-Policy Distillation of LLMs** (`gu2024minillm`), Y. Gu, 2023, arXiv 2306.08543, ICLR 2024.
*Method:* reverse-KL knowledge distillation optimised on-policy, i.e. on the student's own samples.
*Result:* less exposure bias and better calibration than forward-KL KD, from 120M to 13B parameters.
*Relation:* the white-box on-policy alternative to BC on 31B games. It needs 31B logits, which the API does not give, so for us it would need a local 31B. With API only, BC/AWR or DAgger-style relabelling (`ross2011dagger`) is the realistic path.

## (c) Communication and conventions among LLM agents

**Emergent social conventions and collective bias in LLM populations** (`ashery2024conventions`), A. Flint Ashery, 2024, arXiv 2410.08948, preprint.
*Method:* naming-game style pairwise interactions in decentralised LLM populations.
*Result:* universally adopted conventions emerge spontaneously. Collective biases appear even when no individual agent is biased, and committed adversarial minorities can tip the convention.
*Relation:* our "focusing e4" target-call convention is a small-scale case of this. The minority-tipping result motivates a mixed-team test: can one 31B unit set the convention for 4B teammates?

**LLM-MARL: Language-Driven Coordination and Learning in Multi-Agent Simulation Environments** (`li2025llmmarl`), Z. Li, 2025, arXiv 2506.04251, preprint.
*Method:* LLM Coordinator (subgoals), Communicator (symbolic messages) and Memory modules on top of PPO, with a language-conditioned loss.
*Result:* beats MAPPO/QMIX on GRF, MAgent and SC2. Ablations show that subgoals and messages each help.
*Relation:* hybrid (the LLM advises an RL policy), whereas our LLM is the policy. Its messaging ablation agrees with our C0 to C2 gains. The abstract gives no SMACv2 specifics.

## (d) Shaping, credit assignment and reward hacking

**Potential-based shaping** (`ng1999shaping`), Ng, Harada & Russell, ICML 1999. Adding F = γΦ(s') − Φ(s) leaves optimal policies unchanged. *Relation:* our v2 HP+alive-difference reward is "potential-like" only if implemented as a difference of Φ between steps. v1's kill/focus bonuses are not potential-based and can change the optimum, which is a candidate for reward hacking. In the report we should say which variant satisfies the theorem.

**COMA: Counterfactual Multi-Agent Policy Gradients** (`foerster2018coma`), J. Foerster, 2017, arXiv 1705.08926 (no venue in the arXiv comment). A centralised critic gives each agent a counterfactual baseline that marginalises out its own action, and it was evaluated on SC micromanagement. *Relation:* our per-unit "zero-sum credit" is a heuristic stand-in. With an LLM policy, a counterfactual baseline could come from re-sampling one unit's action with the others held fixed.

**Difference rewards / Aristocrat Utility** (`wolpert2001collectives`), Wolpert & Tumer, Advances in Complex Systems 4, 2001. They derive per-agent payoffs (world utility minus utility with the agent's action removed) that beat giving everyone the team utility. *Relation:* the formal basis for our planned individual-vs-team reward study. Pure team reward invites free-riding, and difference rewards fix it in principle.

**CCPO: Counterfactual Credit Policy Optimization** (`li2026ccpo`), Z. Li, 2026, arXiv 2603.21563, preprint.
*Method:* computes each agent's marginal contribution by removing that agent (a counterfactual rollout) and feeds role-specific rewards to GRPO/GSPO/REINFORCE++.
*Result:* often improves dual-agent maths reasoning, especially MATH500, though gains vary.
*Relation:* the LLM-era difference reward. Its motivation ("shared terminal rewards ... can encourage free-riding") is exactly our individual-vs-team question.

**C3: The Trace Is the State** (`chen2026c3`), Y. Chen, 2026, arXiv 2603.06859, preprint.
*Method:* swaps one message at a decision point and continues the rollout to the terminal reward, giving unbiased credit up to Monte Carlo error.
*Result:* with 4 continuations, rank correlation 0.69 against a reference, versus 0.29 for a learned critic. It beats MAGRPO and uses 37% fewer training tokens than MAPPO.
*Relation:* SC2 cannot be rewound cheaply, so exact continuation is costly for us. But the message-level credit idea fits C2: estimate what each `say` message was worth.

**Defining and Characterizing Reward Hacking** (`skalse2022rewardhacking`), J. Skalse, 2022, arXiv 2209.13085, preprint. A proxy is "unhackable" only if raising it never lowers the true return, and over all stochastic policies this holds only trivially. *Relation:* the formal frame for our "reward hacking across models" idea. Our true reward is win/loss, and v1/v2/v2.1 are proxies.

**Natural Emergent Misalignment from Reward Hacking in Production RL** (`macdiarmid2025rewardhacking`), M. MacDiarmid, 2025, arXiv 2511.18397, preprint. Models that learn to hack coding environments generalise to alignment faking and sabotage. Mitigations: block the hack, use more diverse safety training, and "inoculation prompting". *Relation:* motivation only. Our hacks (e.g. farming HP damage without winning) are benign, but this paper shows why measuring them matters.

**Reward Shaping to Mitigate Reward Hacking in RLHF (PAR)** (`fu2025par`), J. Fu, 2025, arXiv 2502.18770, preprint.
*Method/result:* shaped rewards should be bounded and should rise fast then saturate. Their PAR applies a sigmoid to centred rewards, which reduces variance and widens the window for early stopping (Gemma2-2B, PPO).
*Relation:* cheap to adopt. Bound our dense HP rewards before GRPO normalisation, and check whether reward kept rising while win rate stayed flat.

## (e) Free-riding and individual incentives

**Corrupted by Reasoning: Reasoning LMs Become Free-Riders in Public Goods Games** (`piedrahita2025corrupted`), D. Guzman Piedrahita, 2025, arXiv 2506.23276, COLM 2025.
*Method:* a repeated public goods game with costly sanctioning and a choice of institution.
*Result:* four behavioural archetypes. Reasoning models (o1 series) cooperate worse than some traditional LLMs.
*Relation:* predicts that individual-reward RL or stronger reasoning could produce free-riders in our squads (units that avoid fire to keep HP). We can measure this directly with per-unit damage taken and dealt.

**From Lazy Agents to Deliberation** (`zhang2025lazyagents`), Z. Zhang, 2025, arXiv 2511.02303, preprint.
*Method:* a theoretical analysis of why "lazy agent" behaviour (one agent dominates) arises in multi-agent RL reasoning, plus a causal-influence measure and a restart/deliberation reward.
*Result:* reduces laziness and improves reasoning.
*Relation:* the shared-team-reward failure mode to look for in our GRPO runs. Their causal-influence measure parallels our "follow named target" rate (70% vs 45% chance).

## (f) Real-world swarms, satellites, multi-agent harnesses

**Hierarchical and Collaborative LLM-Based Control for Multi-UAV Motion and Communication** (`yan2025hierarchicaluav`), Z. Yan, 2025, arXiv 2506.06532, ICML 2025 ML4Wireless workshop. An LLM on a high-altitude platform does access control and an onboard LLM per UAV plans motion. It reports higher reward, lower cost and fewer collisions than baselines. *Relation:* the same "one LLM per agent" decomposition, plus a coordinator layer that we lack.

**Talk Less, Fly Lighter: Semantic Compression for UAV Swarm Communication via LLMs** (`lin2025talkless`), F. Lin, 2025, arXiv 2508.12043, preprint. Evaluates 9 LLMs that compress inter-UAV messages under bandwidth and multi-hop limits. *Relation:* the aerospace analogue of our ≤8-word cap. Our evidence that 8 words carry useful target calls is a data point on how little bandwidth coordination needs.

**On-Orbit Space AI: Federated, Multi-Agent, and Collaborative Algorithms for Satellite Constellations** (`wang2026onorbit`), Z. Wang, 2026, arXiv 2604.16518, Algorithms (MDPI), accepted. A survey that treats a constellation as a distributed multi-agent system under intermittent links and SWaP-C limits. *Relation:* use it to frame partial observability plus delayed or limited messaging as the shared problem.

**NAVI-Orbital** (`delfa2026naviorbital`), J. M. Delfa Victoria, 2026, arXiv 2606.18271, preprint. The first in-orbit VLM (Gemma 3) running autonomous Earth-observation inference onboard, with LangGraph orchestrating detection and dialogue agents. 88.16% accuracy on the AID benchmark. *Relation:* shows that a small Gemma-class model is flight-feasible, which argues for making the **4B** model coordinate well rather than relying on 31B.

**Beyond detection: cooperative multi-agent reasoning for onboard EO crisis response** (`mousist2026eocrisis`), A. D. Mousist, 2026, arXiv 2603.19858, ESA 4S Symposium 2026. Hierarchical onboard agents (early warning, then specialists, then a decision agent) under bandwidth limits. *Relation:* a hierarchy again. Real systems add a decision layer that our flat peers lack.

**AutoGen** (`wu2023autogen`), Q. Wu, 2023, arXiv 2308.08155, preprint. A conversable multi-agent framework mixing LLMs, tools and humans. *Relation:* a generic harness. Ours is a custom tick-synchronous loop, which is simpler and matches real-time constraints better. Cite it as the standard alternative.

## Where our work sits

- **Novel:** a clean causal ablation of the channel (C0 → C1 → C2) for **per-unit LLM control** on SMACv2, with significance tests. COMPASS, SMAC-Talk and LLM-PySC2 report systems or benchmarks but not this controlled comparison.
- **Novel:** a behavioural analysis of the messages themselves (85% target calls; 70% follow rate vs 45% chance). This is measured evidence that an LLM invents focus fire as a convention, not just that "communication helps".
- **Novel-ish:** the history ablation on a 4B model (19% → 3%) quantifies memory as the bottleneck for small models, consistent with StarWM's and TextStarCraft's history findings.
- **Weakness:** C1 state sharing is close to COMPASS's structured entity communication, and COMPASS's 57% on Protoss 5v5 is similar to our 60%. Our headline is not a SOTA win rate, and our small sample makes the CIs wide.
- **Weakness:** self-play GRPO gave no significant gain (23% vs 17%, p=0.48) after only 64 games. Related work (SPIRAL, Self-RedTeam, MAGRPO) trains far longer, so our null result is underpowered, not evidence that RL fails.
- **Weakness:** v1 shaping is not potential-based (Ng 1999), and our per-unit credit is heuristic rather than counterfactual (COMA, CCPO). The side-A asymmetry (61/88) also confounds self-play groups.
- **Weakness:** we evaluate against one scripted opponent, which SMAC-Hard shows can be exploited. No evaluation against varied opponents yet.
- **Open but timely:** individual-vs-team reward and free-riding in an embodied, real-time LLM team. Prior free-riding work (public goods games, lazy agents, CCPO) is in matrix games or maths.

## Is anyone else using SMAC/SMACv2 with LLMs?

Yes, but few groups, and none with our exact design. On SMACv2: **COMPASS** (VLM, code skills, multi-hop communication; Protoss 5v5 57%) and **SMAC-Talk** (text interface for SMACv2 with a messaging channel, Qwen 4B-122B; already in the bib). On SMAC v1: **SMAC-R1 / LLM-SMAC** (the LLM writes decision-tree code, later SFT+GRPO on Qwen-7B) and LLM-PySC2's LLM-SMAC tasks. **AVACraft** and **HLSMAC** are SMAC-style variants. To our knowledge, none of these combine one LLM call per unit per step, a controlled C0/C1/C2 channel ablation, message-following analysis, and self-play GRPO of a small open model.

## Ideas we could adopt

1. **Make shaping provably potential-based.** Use Φ = (own HP+alive) − (enemy HP+alive) and r' = r_win + γΦ(s') − Φ(s), drop the non-potential kill/focus bonuses, and bound the result (PAR-style sigmoid). Report reward vs win-rate curves to show whether hacking happens.
2. **Counterfactual per-unit credit (COMA/CCPO-lite).** At sampled steps, resample one unit's action K times with teammates fixed and use the mean as that unit's baseline. This also gives the difference reward needed for the individual-vs-team study.
3. **Varied opponents (SMAC-Hard).** Evaluate against a second opponent, e.g. a frozen 31B squad or an edited script, so the gains are not exploits of the built-in AI. Balance self-play sides by swapping A/B within each GRPO group.
4. **Agent distillation from 31B.** Behaviour-clone E4B on winning 31B trajectories, including messages, with first-thought-prefix-style teacher prompting (Agent Distillation). At test time add self-consistent action voting. Run on-policy KD (MiniLLM) only if a local 31B becomes available.
5. **Mixed teams as a convention-tipping test** (Ashery et al.). Use one 31B "caller" plus four 4B units, and measure whether 4B follow rates and win rate rise. This is cheaper than RL and tests whether the convention transfers.
